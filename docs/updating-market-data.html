Updating the market data

The Compare page and every estimate run on prices and reward tables typed into the repo by hand. This is the six-monthly checklist for refreshing them: what to change, where it lives, where to look it up, and how to test and release.

When to do it

Twice a year. MARKET.reviewBy in src/market.js is the deadline: once today is past it, marketAge() reports stale: true and the page can warn that its prices may be old.

WindowWhat changes then
JanuaryMost medical schemes (Bonitas, Momentum, Medshield, Fedhealth, Bestmed) and gap cover insurers change their prices. Discovery Bank fees often change too (the Bank's own note: "new rates usually start now").
JanuaryDiscovery Health's contribution increase too: 2026's was a once-off delay to 1 April, and 2027's starts on 1 January again.
September – NovemberDiscovery Bank fees guides (2026: effective 1 September) and the Vitality Money rewards guides (2026: effective 1 November). Schemes and gap cover insurers announce next year's prices in September and October: add them as y2027 (see Medical aid) so the plugin switches on 1 January.
Rule for every figure
Use the provider's own document. If you can only find a broker's copy or a news article, keep the figure, add unconfirmed: true, and say why in source. If you cannot find a figure at all, leave the old one (or null) and flag it. Never guess a number into the tables.

Every source string names the document and its date, for example 'Bonitas Annexure A 2027 (from 1 Jan 2027)'. The tests fail when a comparison row's source has no year in it. A flagged row also says why in unconfirmedWhy.

Then update market-data-sources.html, the public table of every figure, its value at the source, the link and a verdict. It was built on 3 Nov 2026 by checking each figure in parallel lanes (bank fees and Miles, band tables, Vitality rules, Discovery Health, other schemes, gap/car/life/home, rival banks); repeat that for each refresh.

Discovery Bank

Rewards guides (band tables, base Miles, thresholds)

One PDF per collection, under discovery.co.za/assets/discoverycoza/bank/marketing-material/vitality-money/:

  • understanding-your-dynamic-discounts-rewards-percentages-discovery-gold-card-account-and-discovery-gold-full-banking-suite.pdf
  • …-discovery-platinum-card-account-and-discovery-platinum-full-banking-suite.pdf
  • …-discovery-black-card-account-and-discovery-black-full-banking-suite.pdf
  • …-discovery-purple-full-banking-suite.pdf

Easiest way to read them: download, then pdftotext -layout guide.pdf guide.txt keeps the tables in columns.

  1. Check the cover date ("Effective 1 November 2026") and update the guide constants (BLACK_GUIDE, GOLD_GUIDE …) in rates.js.
  2. For each collection, copy the spend bands (lower bounds) and one row per status (Blue to Diamond) into SUITE_TABLES. The collections do not share bands: Gold stops at R15 250, Purple jumps from R3 150 to R23 600. A table that does not apply is null (Purple has no Credit Card Account).
  3. Local flights have two columns: the Suite (and bundled Transaction Account) and the Credit Card Account. They go into flightsLocal and ccFlightsLocal.
  4. Base Miles: the "Below-threshold virtual card earn rates" table gives baseMiles by status, and the "Monthly virtual card spend threshold" gives milesThreshold. Update both SUITES and BANK_PRODUCTS.
  5. Read the overview page for exceptions, for example "Upgrade to a Gold Suite to unlock HealthyCare" means the Gold Credit Card Account's healthyCare band is null in its BANK_PRODUCTS row.

Fees guides

Under discovery.co.za/wcm/discoverycoza/assets/bank/fees-guides/: discovery-bank-<gold|platinum|black>-suite-fees.pdf, discovery-bank-purple-fees.pdf, discovery-bank-<tier>-credit-card-fees.pdf, discovery-bank-<tier>-transaction-bundled-fees.pdf, discovery-bank-<tier>-transaction-payt-fees.pdf and discovery-account-fees-guide.pdf.

Copy the "Account fee", "Vitality Money premium" and "Single credit facility fee" lines. The account fee is what a statement calls "Monthly account fee", and the onboarding uses it to guess the tier, so keep every product's account fee different (a test checks this).

Single rates

HealthyFood and HealthyCare caps (one person, and the family case), the Vitality Drive fuel percentages, the Apple Watch activation fee and the dynamic interest boosts are in RATES. Dynamic interest comes from the one-pager save-more-and-pay-less-with-dynamic-interest-rates.pdf: one row per kind of product (Suite, Credit Card Account, bundled Transaction Account), the same for every tier, and the pay-as-you-transact Transaction Account by tier (PAYT_BOOST). Purple is not named in it, so Purple lists it in unconfirmed.

Fees guides also carry once-a-year fees: Purple's R4 250 annual card fee is annualFee on its Suite and product rows, and the rule that reads it off a statement is in constants.js.

Rival banks

ALTERNATIVES in rates.js. Fees: each bank's pricing guide (Capitec from 1 March, FNB from 1 July, Standard Bank and Absa from 1 January). Rewards: only a published rate counts. A programme with tiers or levels is counted at a middle tier (Tier 3), and the note says so. FNB pays no eBucks on general card spend, so its backPct is 0. Absa's Bank Earn is a fixed rand amount by spend band: bankEarn. Spend a rival does not reward goes in flatExcludes (UCount's grocers), and flatExcludesPartners stops a partner's spend earning the flat reward as well. A partner rate with pct: null is unpublished or depends on a level we cannot know; the page then shows what rate would match Discovery. Capitec's site blocks downloads: read its fees PDF, and its Live Better page through an archived copy.

Medical aid

  1. Discovery Health: the scheme's plan comparison, discovery.co.za/wcm/discoverycoza/assets/faz/medical-aid/<year>/benefit-information/discovery-health-medical-scheme-plan-comparison.pdf. Use the "Total contributions" columns (savings included). Update the three Discovery rows in HEALTH_PLANS and every row in HEALTH_OWN. KeyCare is priced by income, one row per band, and the bands move each year, so the next year's label goes in too.
  2. Next year's prices, published before they start: add them to the row as y2027: { main, adult, child } (gap cover: { monthly }), with a new plan name, coverNote parts or gapCore where those change. priceAt(row, when) in compare.js switches to them from PERIODS.discoveryHealth2027.from, and every caller passes the day it prices on (the debit's day, or today). Give the period a next link (discoveryHealth → discoveryHealth2027) so the change does not make the data stale. A new plan with no current price has main: null. When the year turns, move the y2027 figures into the row, delete y2027, and rename the field for the next year.
  3. Other schemes: each scheme's own contribution table (Bonitas and Bestmed "Annexure A"). Rows read from a broker's copy keep unconfirmed: true.
  4. Re-read the cover notes (cover and coverNote) for any plan whose benefits changed. Grades are yes, limited, no or unknown.
  5. Update ESSENTIAL_GAP (Discovery Gap Core on Essential plans) from the Discovery Gap Cover brochure.

Gap, car, life and home

  • Gap cover (GAP_PLANS): the monthly premium for a family whose oldest member is under 45, from each insurer's brochure. A product sold only to younger families gets pricedUnder (Zestlife Optimal: 42).
  • Car insurance (INSURERS): only the claim-free reward rule changes; premiums are personal, so users type quotes. Read the reward's base carefully: "10% of the premiums you paid" over three claim-free years is about 10% a year, so rewardPct is 10, not 10 ÷ 3. A paid add-on counts 0.
  • Life (LIFE_OFFERS, LIFE_BENCHMARK): the reward notes and the latest published price range.
  • Home contents (HOME_INSURERS, HOME_RANGE): a published example premium gives per100k; otherwise the row waits for a quote.

Stamp MARKET

When everything is checked, open src/market.js and set:

const MARKET = {
  asOf: '2027-05-03',        // the day you finished checking
  reviewBy: '2027-11-03',    // six months later, or the day before the first price change
  note: 'Discovery Bank rewards as at …, bank fees from …, and 2027 prices for …',
};

The test suite checks that reviewBy is no more than six months after asOf and falls before the first known price change (a period's changes, unless its next period is already in). Also update the header comment's table list if you added a table.

Test and release

  1. Run npm test. tests/market.test.cjs checks the stamp, every Suite's tables (one figure per band, never dropping as spend grows) and that every source is dated. tests/compare.test.cjs checks that every Discovery Health plan can be priced.
  2. Tests that pin an exact figure (for example a Diamond fuel percentage) fail when Discovery changes it. Update the expected value to the new published figure, not the other way round.
  3. Run ./build.sh. It rebuilds main.js and styles.css (build output, never edit them by hand) and runs the whole suite.
  4. Bump the version in manifest.json, package.json and versions.json, commit, then push a tag that equals the version exactly (no "v"). The release workflow rebuilds, checks the committed bundle matches, and publishes main.js, manifest.json and styles.css.
Public repo
Only published prices go into the tables. Never copy a real person's statement figures, names or account numbers into the repo or the tests.
