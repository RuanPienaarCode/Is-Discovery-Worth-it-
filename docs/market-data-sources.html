How to read this

  • Verified The figure in the plugin matches the provider's own document (discovery.co.za, the scheme's or the bank's own site).
  • Was wrong · fixed The plugin had a different figure or rule; it now has the source's, in this release.
  • Third-party only · flagged Only a broker, an aggregator or a news report publishes it, or the provider's own page could not be read. The plugin marks it unconfirmed and the page says to check it.

"In the plugin" is the value in the code, by the name in codeRef (Blue / Bronze / Silver / Gold / Diamond for a status row; main member / adult / child for a medical aid). Checked 3 November 2026. Rates you type in the plugin always win over these.

Not advice
These are published prices and rules. Your own quote, plan rules and statements decide what you actually pay.

How the comparisons work, and what changed

Fixed in this release

  • Car and home claim-free rewards were counted at a third to a half of their value. OUTsurance, MiWay and Santam pay 10% of all the premiums you paid over the claim-free years, not 10% of one year's. That is about 10% a year, so rewardPct is now 10 (it was 3,33 or 5). Dialdirect's home Payback is a paid add-on, so it now counts 0, as the car one already did.
  • Rival banks' rewards. FNB pays no eBucks on general card spend, only at partners (now 0%, with Pick n Pay as a partner whose rate depends on your eBucks level). Standard Bank UCount and Absa Rewards have tiers: the plugin now counts a middle tier (Tier 3) and says so, instead of the top rate. Absa's Bank Earn is a fixed rand amount by spend band, now modelled as one. The same spend is no longer rewarded twice: UCount pays no card rewards at grocers, and Absa's Bank Earn leaves out spend a partner already rewarded. Capitec's figures are now confirmed by Capitec.
  • Discovery Health's price period. The 2026 prices run to 31 December 2026, not 31 March 2027. The 2027 prices are in and take over on 1 January 2027, for the debit-order split, the plan guess and the comparison.
  • Gap cover by age. Zestlife Optimal is sold only when everyone is under 42, so a family whose oldest is 42–44 no longer sees it as a saving.
  • The Ðiscovery Account (the free savings account) comes with Vitality Money and a virtual card. It now unlocks the gameboard, Miles spending and the flight discount, but still no base Miles or lifestyle extras.
  • Flights. Without Vitality Health the bank gives at least 10% off local flights (a flat 10% on a pay-as-you-transact account), now counted. Vitality Health's own flight discount is booked through Vitality Travel, which needs a Discovery Bank account (the free one is enough).
  • Bank extras do not need Vitality Health. Confirmed: the guides have a column for "Vitality Money, no Vitality Health".
  • HealthyFood partners are Checkers and Woolworths only. Pick n Pay was named in three sentences; the sums were already right.
  • Vitality Health's spend limits are R2 500 (HealthyFood) and R2 000 (HealthyCare) for one person, R5 000 and R4 000 for a family. The plugin used the family limits for everyone; it now uses your household.
  • Purple Suite's R4 250 annual card fee (refunded only with an average daily balance of R1 million) is now named and counted when a statement shows it.
  • Dynamic interest is set by the kind of account, not the tier: the Gold and Platinum figures are now confirmed, and pay-as-you-transact Transaction Accounts get their own smaller steps.

Still open (named on the page, not changed)

  • Rival banks' per-transaction fees (debit orders, payments) and FNB's R69 credit facility fee in months you use the credit are not counted. Discovery's cheaper-account path does count its own.
  • Absa's partner rates are applied to credit and debit card spend alike; Absa pays less on debit cards. Absa's Clicks partner is not modelled.
  • Gap cover: some insurers charge families under 28 or 30 less (Ambledown, Sanlam), and who sets the age differs (the oldest person, the policyholder, the age at entry). Sirago's price covers four dependants. Type a quote if yours differs.
  • The life benchmark is a broker's estimate for ages 40 and 45, and insurers' own examples are often far lower. A quote beats it.
  • After your three return flights, the bank's unlimited 10% off further flights is not counted.
  • Two spouses with qualifying cards get higher bank HealthyFood and HealthyCare limits (R5 000 and R4 000). Type them if that is you.
